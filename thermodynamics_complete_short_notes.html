MODULE 01

Basic Concepts & Terminology

Notebook Page 1

1. System, Surrounding & Boundary

Pg 1
  • System: Specified part of the universe under study.
    Ex: A gas enclosed in a container, water kept in a vessel, a small volume of air in front of you, etc.
  • Surrounding: Rest of the universe except the system.
  • Boundary: Anything that separates the system from the surrounding.

Boundary Can Be:

• Real or Imaginary
• Rigid or Flexible
• Conducting (Diathermic) or Non-conducting (Adiabatic)
• Porous or Non-porous

2. Types of Systems (Based on Boundary)

Pg 1 Table

Depending on the type of boundary, systems are divided into three categories:

Feature Isolated System Closed System Open System
Boundary Type Non-porous & Non-conducting Non-porous & Conducting Porous & Conducting
Transfer of Heat ✗ (No) ✓ (Yes) ✓ (Yes)
Transfer of Mass ✗ (No) ✗ (No) ✓ (Yes)
Example Ideal thermos flask Closed metallic vessel having ideal gas Open metallic vessel having some air

State of System

Set of values of all properties of system required to describe it completely. E.g., $P, V$ and $T$ for an enclosed gas specifies its state.

Thermodynamic Process & Path

Process: Change in the state of system.
Path of process: Sequence of state variables through which system passes.

3. State vs Path Variables

Pg 1
State Variable (State Function)

Value depends only on the current state of system and NOT on the path taken to reach that state.

Ex: Temperature ($T$), Pressure ($P$), Volume ($V$), Internal Energy ($U$), Entropy ($S$).

Path Variable (Path Function)

Value depends on the path taken by the system.

Ex: Heat supplied ($Q$), Work done ($W$), Molar Heat Capacity ($C$).

4. Thermodynamic Equilibrium

• Thermal Equilibrium: Temperature ($T$) is same everywhere in system $\implies$ no net heat flow inside system.
• Mechanical Equilibrium: Pressure ($P$) is same everywhere inside system $\implies$ no net flow of mass inside system.

5. Intensive vs Extensive Properties

Pg 1
Intensive Property (Independent of Mass)

Value does NOT depend on the quantity of matter present in the system.

Ex: Density ($\rho$), Temperature ($T$), Pressure ($P$), Specific heat ($s, C$), etc.

Extensive Property (Depends on Mass)

Value depends on the quantity of matter present in the system.

Ex: Mass ($m$), Volume ($V$), Internal Energy ($U$), Number of moles ($n$), etc.

💡 Partition Test Trick (From Notes):

Imagine dividing the system into two parts with a partition:
• If the value of the property changes (halves) $\implies$ Extensive.
• If the value remains unchanged $\implies$ Intensive.

6. Processes, Quasi-Static & Reversibility

Pg 1
5 Basic Thermodynamic Processes:
1. Isothermal: Temperature remains constant ($T = \text{const}$)
2. Isochoric: Volume remains constant ($V = \text{const}$)
3. Isobaric: Pressure remains constant ($P = \text{const}$)
4. Adiabatic: No heat exchange with surrounding ($Q = 0$)
5. Cyclic: Initial and final states are same ($\Delta U_{\text{cycle}} = 0$)
Quasi-Static Process (5 Key Points):
  • Infinitely slow process
  • Takes infinite time to complete
  • Driving force is infinitesimally small
  • System is always in thermodynamic equilibrium
  • State of system is always well-defined at every point
Reversible & Irreversible Process:

If a process can be reversed and both system as well as surrounding can be restored to their original state, it is called Reversible.

Conditions for Reversible Process:
  1. It must be quasi-static.
  2. There are no dissipative forces like friction or viscosity.

*(If a process is not reversible, then it is irreversible.)*

Pg 2

$\implies \mathbf{dW = \text{-ve}}$ ($W < 0$)
• Work done ON the gas (by surrounding).

2. Internal Energy ($U$), Heat ($Q$) & 1st Law (FLOT)

Pg 3–4
Internal Energy & Its Change State Function
$$U = \frac{f}{2}nRT = \frac{f}{2}PV \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad \Delta U = \frac{f}{2}nR\Delta T = \frac{f}{2}\Delta(PV)$$
• Gas Heated: $\Delta T = \text{+ve} \implies \mathbf{\Delta U = \text{+ve}}$
• Gas Cooled: $\Delta T = \text{-ve} \implies \mathbf{\Delta U = \text{-ve}}$
Heat Supplied ($Q$) & Molar Specific Heat ($C$) Path Function
• Heat added to system: $\mathbf{Q = \text{+ve}}$
• Heat removed from system: $\mathbf{Q = \text{-ve}}$

Molar Specific Heat Capacity ($C$): Amount of heat required for unit rise in temperature of $1\text{ mole}$ of substance.

$$C = \frac{Q}{n\Delta T} \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad Q = nC\Delta T$$
  • • For an ideal gas, infinite values of $C$ are possible (depending on the process). '$C$' can be $\text{+ve}$, $\text{-ve}$, zero, or $\infty$.
  • • Crucial Trap Note: Increasing the temperature of gas does NOT necessarily imply that heat is supplied to the gas! If $C$ is negative, then $Q$ is negative even when $\Delta T$ is positive (and vice versa).
1st Law of Thermodynamics (FLOT) — Page 4 Based on LOCE

Heat supplied to a system ($Q$) is equal to the sum of change in internal energy ($\Delta U$) and work done by the system ($W$). FLOT is nothing but the Law of Conservation of Energy (LOCE).

$$\boxed{Q = \Delta U + W} \qquad \text{or} \qquad \boxed{dQ = dU + dW}$$

Process A • Pages 4–6

Isothermal Process ($T = \text{constant}$)

$\Delta T = 0,\; \Delta U = 0$

Process in which temperature of gas remains constant ($T = \text{const} \implies \Delta T = 0$). Hence internal energy of system also remains constant ($U = \text{const} \implies \Delta U = 0$).

Equation of State: $$PV = nRT = \text{constant} \implies P_i V_i = P_f V_f$$
Slope of Isotherm: $$\frac{dP}{dV} = -\frac{P}{V}$$
Work Done (All 4 Forms from Page 5): $$W = nRT \ln\left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right) = nRT \ln\left(\frac{P_i}{P_f}\right) = P_i V_i \ln\left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right) = P_f V_f \ln\left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right)$$ $$W = 2.303\,nRT \log_{10}\left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right)$$
FLOT & Molar Specific Heat ($C$): $$\Delta U = 0 \implies Q = W = nRT \ln\left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right) \qquad \Big| \qquad C = \frac{Q}{n\Delta T} = \frac{Q}{0} = \infty$$
• $P$-$V$ graph of an isothermal process is a rectangular hyperbola (called an Isotherm).
• Farther is the isotherm from the origin, higher is the Temperature ($T_2 > T_1$).
📐 View Step-by-Step Derivations (Slope & Work Done — Page 5) ▼
1. Slope of Isotherm: Differentiate $PV = \text{constant}$ w.r.t. $V$: $$P(1) + V\frac{dP}{dV} = 0 \implies \frac{dP}{dV} = -\frac{P}{V}$$
2. Work Done: Using $PV = nRT \implies P = \frac{nRT}{V}$: $$W = \int_{V_i}^{V_f} P\,dV = \int_{V_i}^{V_f} \frac{nRT}{V}\,dV = nRT \ln\left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right)$$ Since $\ln x = \frac{\log_{10} x}{\log_{10} e} = 2.303 \log_{10} x \implies W = 2.303\,nRT\log_{10}\left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right)$.
Process B • Pages 6–7

Isochoric Process ($V = \text{constant}$)

$\Delta V = 0,\; W = 0$

Process in which volume of gas remains constant ($V = \text{const} \implies \Delta V = 0 \implies dW = P\,dV = 0 \implies W = 0$).

Equation of State: $$PV = nRT \implies P \propto T \implies \frac{P_i}{P_f} = \frac{T_i}{T_f}$$
FLOT & Molar Heat Capacity at Constant Volume ($C_V$): $$W = 0 \implies Q = \Delta U = \frac{f}{2}nR\Delta T$$ $$C_V = \left.\frac{Q}{n\Delta T}\right|_{V=\text{const}} = \frac{\frac{f}{2}nR\Delta T}{n\Delta T} \implies \boxed{C_V = \frac{f}{2}R}$$
★ Universal Rule (Page 7):
• $\Delta U = nC_V\Delta T$ $\longrightarrow$ Valid FOR ANY PROCESS!
• $Q = nC_V\Delta T$ $\longrightarrow$ Valid ONLY FOR ISOCHORIC PROCESS! ($Q = nC\Delta T$ is for any process).
$P$-$V$ Graph: Vertical straight line ($V = \text{const}$). Area under curve $= 0 \implies W = 0$.
$P$-$T$ Graph: $P = \left(\frac{nR}{V}\right)T$
Slope $m = \frac{nR}{V} \propto \frac{1}{V}$
$m_1 < m_2 \implies V_1 > V_2$
Monoatomic ($f=3$)
$C_V = \frac{3}{2}R$
Diatomic / Linear ($f=5$)
$C_V = \frac{5}{2}R$
Non-linear Poly ($f=6$)
$C_V = \frac{6}{2}R = 3R$
Process C • Pages 8–9

Isobaric Process ($P = \text{constant}$)

$\Delta P = 0,\; W = P\Delta V$

Process in which pressure of gas remains constant ($P = \text{const} \implies \Delta P = 0$).

Equation of State: $$PV = nRT \implies V \propto T \implies \frac{V_i}{V_f} = \frac{T_i}{T_f}$$
Work Done: $$W = \int_{V_i}^{V_f} P\,dV = P(V_f - V_i) = P\Delta V = nR\Delta T$$
FLOT & Molar Specific Heat at Constant Pressure ($C_P$): $$\Delta U = \frac{f}{2}nR\Delta T = nC_V\Delta T$$ $$Q = \Delta U + W = \frac{f}{2}nR\Delta T + nR\Delta T = \left(\frac{f}{2} + 1\right)nR\Delta T = nC_P\Delta T$$ $$C_P = \left.\frac{Q}{n\Delta T}\right|_{P=\text{const}} = \boxed{\left(\frac{f}{2} + 1\right)R}$$
$P$-$V$ Graph: Horizontal line ($P = \text{const}$). Rectangle area $= P(V_f - V_i)$.
$V$-$T$ Graph: $V = \left(\frac{nR}{P}\right)T$
Slope $m = \frac{nR}{P} \propto \frac{1}{P}$
$m_1 < m_2 \implies P_1 > P_2$
Monoatomic ($f=3$)
$C_P = \frac{5}{2}R$
Diatomic / Linear ($f=5$)
$C_P = \frac{7}{2}R$
Non-linear Poly ($f=6$)
$C_P = \frac{8}{2}R = 4R$
Key Relations • Pages 9–10

Mayer's Relation & Adiabatic Exponent ($\gamma$)

$C_P - C_V = R$
Mayer's Relation: Since $C_P = \left(\frac{f}{2}+1\right)R$ and $C_V = \frac{f}{2}R$: $$\boxed{C_P - C_V = R} \qquad (\text{Always } C_P > C_V)$$
Adiabatic Exponent ($\gamma$): Defined as the ratio of $C_P$ and $C_V$: $$\gamma = \frac{C_P}{C_V} = \frac{\left(\frac{f}{2}+1\right)R}{\frac{f}{2}R} \implies \boxed{\gamma = 1 + \frac{2}{f}}$$
$C_V$ and $C_P$ in terms of $\gamma$: ($C_P = \gamma C_V \implies \gamma C_V - C_V = R$) $$\boxed{C_V = \frac{R}{\gamma - 1}} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \boxed{C_P = \frac{\gamma R}{\gamma - 1}}$$
Atomicity (DOF $f$) $C_V = \frac{f}{2}R$ $C_P = \left(\frac{f}{2}+1\right)R$ $\gamma = 1 + \frac{2}{f} = \frac{C_P}{C_V}$
Monoatomic ($f = 3$) $\frac{3}{2}R = 1.5R$ $\frac{5}{2}R = 2.5R$ $\frac{5}{3} = 1.67$
Diatomic / Linear Polyatomic ($f = 5$) $\frac{5}{2}R = 2.5R$ $\frac{7}{2}R = 3.5R$ $\frac{7}{5} = 1.4$
Non-linear Polyatomic ($f = 6$) $\frac{6}{2}R = 3R$ $\frac{8}{2}R = 4R$ $\frac{8}{6} = \frac{4}{3} = 1.33$
Process D • Pages 10, 11 & 13

Adiabatic Process ($Q = 0$, Isolated Systems)

$Q = 0,\; \Delta U = -W,\; PV^\gamma = \text{const}$

Process in which there is no heat exchange with surrounding ($Q = 0$). Such processes happen in isolated systems.

3 Forms of Equation of State (Page 11): $$\boxed{PV^\gamma = \text{constant}} \qquad \boxed{TV^{\gamma - 1} = \text{constant}} \qquad \boxed{P^{1 - \gamma}T^\gamma = \text{constant}}$$
Slope of Adiabat vs Slope of Isotherm: $$\text{Slope of Adiabat} = \frac{dP}{dV} = -\gamma\left(\frac{P}{V}\right) = \gamma \times (\text{Slope of Isotherm})$$ • In magnitude: $\big|\text{Slope of adiabat}\big| > \big|\text{Slope of isotherm}\big|$ (since $\gamma > 1$)
• Algebraically (with $-\text{ve}$ sign): $(\text{Slope of adiabat}) < (\text{Slope of isotherm})$
• Conclusion: At a given point, an adiabat is STEEPER than an isotherm.
Internal Energy, Work Done & Specific Heat ($C$): $$\Delta U = nC_V\Delta T = \frac{f}{2}nR\Delta T \qquad \Big| \qquad Q = 0 \implies W = -\Delta U = -nC_V\Delta T$$ $$W = \frac{nR\Delta T}{1 - \gamma} = \frac{\Delta(PV)}{1 - \gamma} = \frac{P_f V_f - P_i V_i}{1 - \gamma} = \frac{P_i V_i - P_f V_f}{\gamma - 1}$$ $$C = \frac{Q}{n\Delta T} = \mathbf{0}$$
📐 View Full Calculus Derivation of $PV^\gamma = \text{const}$ & Slope (Pages 10–11) ▼
Step 1: From FLOT, $dQ = dU + dW = 0 \implies nC_V\,dT + P\,dV = 0 \quad \text{--- (1)}$
Step 2: Differentiating $PV = nRT \implies P\,dV + V\,dP = nR\,dT \implies n\,dT = \frac{P\,dV + V\,dP}{R}$
Step 3: Putting $n\,dT$ in (1): $$C_V\left[\frac{P\,dV + V\,dP}{R}\right] + P\,dV = 0 \implies (C_V + R)P\,dV + C_V V\,dP = 0$$ Since $C_P = C_V + R$: $\quad C_P P\,dV + C_V V\,dP = 0$.
Step 4: Divide throughout by $C_V PV$: $$\frac{C_P}{C_V}\frac{dV}{V} + \frac{dP}{P} = 0 \implies \gamma\int\frac{dV}{V} + \int\frac{dP}{P} = C$$ $$\gamma\ln V + \ln P = C \implies \ln(PV^\gamma) = C \implies \boxed{PV^\gamma = \text{constant}}$$
Step 5 (Other forms): Substitute $P = \frac{nRT}{V} \implies \left(\frac{nRT}{V}\right)V^\gamma = \text{const} \implies TV^{\gamma-1} = \text{const}$. Substitute $V = \frac{nRT}{P} \implies P\left(\frac{nRT}{P}\right)^\gamma = \text{const} \implies P^{1-\gamma}T^\gamma = \text{const}$.
Step 6 (Slope): Differentiate $PV^\gamma = \text{const}$ w.r.t. $V$: $$P(\gamma V^{\gamma-1}) + V^\gamma\frac{dP}{dV} = 0 \implies \frac{dP}{dV} = -\frac{\gamma P}{V}$$

Process E • Page 12

Polytropic Process ($PV^x = \text{constant}$)

$C = C_V + \frac{R}{1-x}$

Any process which fits in the format $\mathbf{PV^x = \text{constant}}$, where $x$ is any real number ($\text{+ve}, \text{-ve}, 0$, integer, or fractional), is called a Polytropic Process.

Equations of State (Similar to Adiabatic): $$PV^x = \text{constant} \qquad \Big| \qquad TV^{x-1} = \text{constant} \qquad \Big| \qquad P^{1-x}T^x = \text{constant}$$
Work Done, $\Delta U$, Heat $Q$ & Molar Specific Heat ($C$): $$W = \frac{\Delta(PV)}{1 - x} = \frac{nR\Delta T}{1 - x} \qquad \Big| \qquad \Delta U = nC_V\Delta T$$ $$Q = \Delta U + W = nC_V\Delta T + \frac{nR\Delta T}{1 - x} = nC\Delta T$$ $$\boxed{C = C_V + \frac{R}{1 - x} = \frac{R}{\gamma - 1} + \frac{R}{1 - x}}$$

1) $PV^2 = \text{const} \implies \mathbf{x = 2}$
2) $\frac{P}{V^2} = \text{const} \implies PV^{-2} = \text{const} \implies \mathbf{x = -2}$
3) $P\sqrt{V} = \text{const} \implies PV^{1/2} = \text{const} \implies \mathbf{x = \frac{1}{2}}$

6) $V^2 T^3 = \text{const} \implies V^2\left(\frac{PV}{nR}\right)^3 = \text{const} \implies P^3 V^5 = \text{const} \implies PV^{5/3} = \text{const} \implies \mathbf{x = \frac{5}{3}}$

1. Mixture of Gases (Effective Properties)

Pg 15

Consider a mixture of two gases: Gas 1 $(M_{01}, n_1, C_{V1}, C_{P1}, \gamma_1)$ and Gas 2 $(M_{02}, n_2, C_{V2}, C_{P2}, \gamma_2)$.

1. Effective Molar Mass $(M_0)_{\text{eff}}$: $$(M_0)_{\text{eff}} = \frac{\text{Total Mass}}{\text{Total Moles}} = \frac{n_1 M_{01} + n_2 M_{02}}{n_1 + n_2}$$
2. Effective $C_V$ (from $\Delta U = \Delta U_1 + \Delta U_2$): $$(n_1 + n_2)(C_V)_{\text{eff}}\Delta T = n_1 C_{V1}\Delta T + n_2 C_{V2}\Delta T \implies \boxed{(C_V)_{\text{eff}} = \frac{n_1 C_{V1} + n_2 C_{V2}}{n_1 + n_2}}$$
3. Effective $C_P$: $$\boxed{(C_P)_{\text{eff}} = \frac{n_1 C_{P1} + n_2 C_{P2}}{n_1 + n_2}}$$
4. Effective Adiabatic Exponent ($\gamma_{\text{eff}}$): $$\frac{R}{\gamma_{\text{eff}} - 1} = \frac{n_1\frac{R}{\gamma_1 - 1} + n_2\frac{R}{\gamma_2 - 1}}{n_1 + n_2} \implies \boxed{\frac{n_1 + n_2}{\gamma_{\text{eff}} - 1} = \frac{n_1}{\gamma_1 - 1} + \frac{n_2}{\gamma_2 - 1}}$$

2. Entropy ($S$) & Second Law of Thermodynamics

Pg 15–16
  • • Definition: Entropy of a system is defined as the measure of randomness in the system. Higher is randomness $\implies$ higher is entropy.
  • Ex: $\text{Entropy of } 1\text{ kg Steam} \gg 1\text{ kg Water} > 1\text{ kg Ice}$.
  • • State Function & 3rd Law: Entropy is a state function. The absolute value of entropy can be calculated using the Third Law of Thermodynamics.
Change in Entropy ($dS$) when small heat $dQ$ is added at temperature $T$:
$$\boxed{dS = \frac{dQ}{T}} \qquad \left(dS \propto dQ, \quad dS \propto \frac{1}{T}, \quad \text{SI Unit} = \text{J/K}\right)$$
Second Law of Thermodynamics (Entropy Statement — Page 16):
  • Entropy of an isolated system cannot be decreased ($\Delta S_{\text{isolated}} \ge 0$).
  • Entropy is NOT conserved.
  • As the universe is an isolated system, the entropy of the universe can never decrease. Once created, entropy can never be destroyed and the universe has to carry it forever.
Entropy Change During Specific Processes (Page 16):
1. Melting (at $T_m$)
$$\Delta S = \int\frac{dQ}{T_m} = \frac{Q}{T_m} = \frac{mL_f}{T_m}$$
2. Boiling (at $T_B$)
$$\Delta S = \int\frac{dQ}{T_B} = \frac{Q}{T_B} = \frac{mL_v}{T_B}$$
3. Heating of Solid

1. Heat Engine & Thermal Efficiency ($\eta$)

Pg 17

• Purpose: Converts heat into usable mechanical work.

3 Essential Components:
  1. Working substance (e.g., ideal gas)
  2. Source of heat (at high temp $T_H$)
  3. Sink of heat (at low temp $T_L$)

• Engine absorbs heat $Q_H$ from source, converts part of it into work $W$, and rejects remaining part $Q_L$ into sink.

★ Rejection of heat ($Q_L$) is necessary for the engine to achieve its original state!

By Conservation of Energy: $$Q_H = Q_L + W \implies W = Q_H - Q_L$$
Efficiency ($\eta$): $$\eta = \frac{\text{Desired Output}}{\text{Required Input}} = \frac{W}{Q_H} = \frac{Q_H - Q_L}{Q_H} = \boxed{1 - \frac{Q_L}{Q_H}}$$

2. Carnot's Engine & Carnot's Theorem

Pg 17–18
Works on a Reversible Cycle with 4 Processes:
a) $A \to B$: Isothermal expansion at $T_H$ (absorbs $Q_H$)
b) $B \to C$: Adiabatic expansion ($T_H \to T_L$)
c) $C \to D$: Isothermal compression at $T_L$ (rejects $Q_L$)
d) $D \to A$: Adiabatic compression ($T_L \to T_H$)
Carnot Cycle Heat Ratio & Efficiency: $$\frac{Q_H}{Q_L} = \frac{T_H}{T_L} \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad \boxed{\eta_{\text{Carnot}} = 1 - \frac{Q_L}{Q_H} = 1 - \frac{T_L}{T_H}} \quad (0 \le \eta < 1)$$
📐 View 5-Equation Derivation of $\frac{Q_H}{Q_L} = \frac{T_H}{T_L}$ (Page 18) ▼
• Isothermal $A \to B$: $Q_H = nRT_H \ln\left(\frac{V_B}{V_A}\right) \quad \text{--- (1)}$
• Isothermal $C \to D$ (magnitude): $Q_L = nRT_L \ln\left(\frac{V_C}{V_D}\right) \quad \text{--- (2)}$
• Adiabatic $B \to C$: $T_H V_B^{\gamma - 1} = T_L V_C^{\gamma - 1} \quad \text{--- (3)}$
• Adiabatic $D \to A$: $T_H V_A^{\gamma - 1} = T_L V_D^{\gamma - 1} \quad \text{--- (4)}$
• From (3) $\div$ (4): $\left(\frac{V_B}{V_A}\right)^{\gamma-1} = \left(\frac{V_C}{V_D}\right)^{\gamma-1} \implies \frac{V_B}{V_A} = \frac{V_C}{V_D} \quad \text{--- (5)}$
• From (1), (2), and (5): $\boxed{\frac{Q_H}{Q_L} = \frac{T_H}{T_L}}$
📜 Carnot's Theorem (Page 18):

All reversible heat engines operating between two given temperatures have the same efficiency, and the efficiency of any engine (reversible or irreversible) operating between the same temperatures cannot exceed Carnot's value.

Solved Notebook Numerical • Page 18

Q: Consider a Carnot's engine operating between $T_H = 600\text{ K}$ & $T_L = 200\text{ K}$. Find work done by engine in each cycle, efficiency & heat rejected to sink in each cycle if engine absorbs $Q_H = 3000\text{ J}$ of heat in each cycle.

1. Efficiency ($\eta$): $$\eta = 1 - \frac{T_L}{T_H} = 1 - \frac{200}{600} = \frac{2}{3} \approx \mathbf{66.67\%}$$
2. Heat Rejected to Sink ($Q_L$): $$\frac{Q_L}{Q_H} = \frac{T_L}{T_H} \implies \frac{Q_L}{3000} = \frac{200}{600} \implies \mathbf{Q_L = 1000\text{ J}}$$
3. Work Done per Cycle ($W$): $$W = Q_H - Q_L = 3000 - 1000 = \mathbf{2000\text{ J}}$$

3. Heat Pump (HP) & Refrigerator (Ref)

Pg 19–20

• HP or Refrigerator removes heat ($Q_L$) from a colder object and dumps heat ($Q_H$) into a hotter surrounding by doing external work $W$.

• Target of Refrigerator: Removal of heat ($Q_L$) from colder space.
• Target of Heat Pump: Dumping of heat ($Q_H$) into hotter surrounding.

• By Conservation of Energy: $\mathbf{Q_L + W = Q_H \implies W = Q_H - Q_L}$.

• For reverse Carnot cycle: $\mathbf{\frac{Q_H}{Q_L} = \frac{T_H}{T_L}}$.

1. Coefficient of Performance of Refrigerator $(\text{COP})_{\text{ref}}$: $$(\text{COP})_{\text{ref}} = \frac{\text{Desired Output}}{\text{Required Input}} = \frac{Q_L}{W} = \frac{Q_L}{Q_H - Q_L} = \frac{T_L}{T_H - T_L}$$ • $(\text{COP})_{\text{ref}} > 0$ (can be less than $1$ or greater than $1$).
2. Coefficient of Performance of Heat Pump $(\text{COP})_{\text{HP}}$: $$(\text{COP})_{\text{HP}} = \frac{\text{Desired Output}}{\text{Required Input}} = \frac{Q_H}{W} = \frac{Q_H}{Q_H - Q_L} = \frac{T_H}{T_H - T_L}$$ • Always $(\text{COP})_{\text{HP}} > 1$.
3. Relation Between $(\text{COP})_{\text{HP}}$ & $(\text{COP})_{\text{ref}}$ (at same temperatures — Page 20): $$\boxed{(\text{COP})_{\text{HP}} = 1 + (\text{COP})_{\text{ref}}}$$

4. Classical Statements of 2nd Law (SLOT)

Pg 20
1. Kelvin–Planck Statement of SLOT No 100% Efficient Engine

According to the Kelvin–Planck statement, it is impossible to construct a heat engine working in cycles to extract heat from a source and convert it completely into work. Some heat ($Q_L > 0$) must always be rejected to the sink ($\eta < 100\%$).

2. Clausius Statement of SLOT No Workless Refrigerator

According to the Clausius statement of SLOT, it is impossible to design a refrigerator working in a cycle whose sole (only) result is the transfer of heat from a colder object to a hotter object. Some external work ($W > 0$) must be done in the process.

💡 In other words: Heat never flows spontaneously from a colder object to a hotter object.
